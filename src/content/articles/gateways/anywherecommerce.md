AnywhereCommerce shows up on merchant statements as a device name, not a provider. A processor sold you an AnyPay app and a reader or an AC Glass terminal; AnywhereCommerce built them. This page explains what the company makes, what it does not do, and how to evaluate the account attached to the hardware.

## What AnywhereCommerce makes

From its site as of September 2026:

| Product | Type |
|---|---|
| AC Glass Mini | Pocket-sized standalone terminal |
| AC Glass 50 | Standalone terminal with integrated printer |
| AC Glass 5500 | Standalone terminal |
| AC Glass Tab | Countertop terminal |
| Walker C3X, Nomad WP3 | Bluetooth card readers for phones and tablets |
| AC MP2, AC MP3 | Mobile receipt printers, 2 and 3 inch |
| AC CP | Countertop printer |
| AnyPay | iOS and Android app: payments, sales tracking, employee management, reporting, custom branding |

It sells to ISOs, developers and merchants across 15 countries, from single locations to multi-location operations. No pricing is published.

## What it is not

Not a processor and not a gateway. The device authorizes through the processor your ISO boarded you with. That means:

- Your rate, monthly fees, PCI fee and contract term are the ISO's.
- Dual pricing, surcharging or cash discount programs depend on the ISO's processor configuration.
- Support for the account is the ISO; support for the hardware is usually routed through the ISO as well.

## Evaluating an AnyPay or AC Glass account

Same three questions as any terminal deal:

1. **Hardware terms.** Placed at no charge, purchased, or leased? If leased, what is the total of all payments and can it be cancelled? Mobile terminal leases at $30 to $50 a month for 36 to 48 months are common and are the most expensive line on many statements.
2. **Processing.** Flat, tiered or interchange-plus, and the effective rate on last month's statement.
3. **Contract.** Term, auto-renewal, early termination fee.

The method for pulling the effective rate is on the [statement guide](/guides/how-to-read-a-merchant-statement).

## The comparison we would put next to it

For mobile and field businesses, a Clover Flex (cellular, battery, printer, customer-facing dual pricing display) or a Dejavoo wireless unit, placed through a free-equipment program for qualifying merchants, with dual pricing configured so in-person processing costs the merchant nothing, and interchange-plus on any keyed or online channel. Month to month. For trades that also invoice, text-to-pay through Hyfin with QuickBooks sync covers the rest. The [contractors and service page](/industries/contractors-and-service) lays out that setup.

If your AnyPay account is on a fair rate with owned hardware and no contract, keep it. The hardware is fine. The account is what to check.
