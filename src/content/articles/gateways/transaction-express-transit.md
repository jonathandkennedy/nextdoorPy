If you came here trying to log in to Transaction Express, the short version is this: the old virtual terminal is at end of life, and Global Payments is moving its users to the TransIT Merchant Center and WebPASS virtual terminal. This page tells you where to log in now, who to call, what to save before your old access ends, and whether following Global Payments to TransIT is the right move. It is written by Jonathan Kennedy, who has spent more than 21 years in payments, and it reflects what we verified on October 19, 2026.

## Trying to log in to Transaction Express? Start here

When we checked on October 19, 2026, the Transaction Express login page at vt.transactionexpress.com was still online. Above the login box it shows a notice titled "Virtual Terminal End of Life" that says the Transaction Express Virtual Terminal "will be decommissioned soon." The notice gives these instructions, quoted closely:

- Your admin user will receive a Welcome email and credentials for **TransIT Merchant Center/WebPASS**.
- Once the Welcome email arrives, you have **5 days to log in** with the temporary password and **30 days to begin processing** through TransIT, or you lose the ability to process transactions.
- After the transition, you no longer have access to the Transaction Express Virtual Terminal.
- Export or download any reporting you need before the portal is decommissioned, and register with **TransLink** for future reporting.
- If you have Customer, Wallet or Recurring records, call **800-543-5327** to coordinate their export or migration.

### Where to log in now

| What you need | Where it is now | Help line |
|---|---|---|
| Key a card payment (virtual terminal) | TransIT Merchant Center and WebPASS at merchantcenter.transit-pass.com | 800-543-5327 (number on the end of life notice) |
| First login to TransIT | Use the temporary password from the Welcome email, or the "Activate" link for new merchants on the same page | 800-543-5327 |
| Deposits, statements and processing reports | TransLink at translink.transfirst.com (register if you have not) | 800-654-9256 for TransLink login and password help |
| Old Transaction Express history | vt.transactionexpress.com, only while your access lasts | 800-543-5327 |

We verified each of those login pages was live on October 19, 2026. Bookmark them from your own browser history or from the Welcome email rather than from a search ad.

### If you cannot get in

- **No Welcome email.** Call 800-543-5327 and confirm which email address is set as your admin user. Check spam. The 5-day clock starts when the email arrives, so do not let it sit.
- **Temporary password expired.** Use "Forgot Password?" on the TransIT Merchant Center login page, or call the same number to have it reissued.
- **Transaction Express no longer accepts your login.** Your account has likely been moved. Go to the TransIT Merchant Center. If you have no TransIT credentials, call 800-543-5327 the same day, because the notice ties your ability to process to starting on TransIT within 30 days.
- **You need a payment taken today.** If you have a card terminal on the same merchant account, you can key the sale there while you sort out access.

## What changed and when

Transaction Express has changed owners and platforms several times. This is the timeline as far as we can verify it.

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| April 1, 2016 | TSYS completes its acquisition of TransFirst, which owned Transaction Express | TSYS press release, April 1, 2016, as reported by PYMNTS |
| September 18, 2019 | Global Payments completes its merger with TSYS | Global Payments press release, September 18, 2019 |
| July 31, 2023 | Global Payments sunsets the TXP gateway and automatically moves merchants to a TransIT proxy, with reporting and a virtual terminal in the Merchant Center | Global Payments merchant notice, as summarized by Merchant Cost Consulting |
| 2026 | The Transaction Express virtual terminal login shows an end of life notice directing users to TransIT Merchant Center and WebPASS | vt.transactionexpress.com, checked October 19, 2026 |

The 2023 notice, per Merchant Cost Consulting's summary, said merchants had to acknowledge the transition to keep service, that 90 days of gateway history would move to TransIT, that merchants would have 31 days afterward to reach their old TXP data, that all active customer profiles would migrate, and that expired wallets would not.

A note on the October 1, 2026 date. The September version of this page gave October 1, 2026 as the retirement date for the virtual terminal. We have not found that date in a public Global Payments document, and the old login page was still online on October 19 with a notice that says "soon" and gives no date. Our reading is that accounts are being moved in waves, each with its own 5-day and 30-day window. Treat the date on your own Welcome email as the one that counts.

## What you lose access to, and how to get it back

When the Transaction Express virtual terminal goes away for your account, three things are at risk.

### Transaction reporting

Gateway-level history (each sale, refund and void, with the card type and who keyed it) lives in Transaction Express until your access ends. Export everything you may need for bookkeeping, sales tax and chargebacks. Card disputes can arrive months after the sale, so pull at least the last 18 months if the system lets you.

If your access is already gone:

- Your monthly processing statements and deposit detail are in **TransLink**, which is the reporting portal the end of life notice points to.
- Call 800-543-5327 and ask for a transaction history export for a specific date range.
- Your bank statements show every deposit by date, which is often enough to answer an accountant's question.

### Stored customers and wallets

Saved cards are held by the gateway, not by you. The notice tells merchants with Customer or Wallet records to call 800-543-5327 to coordinate export or migration. If you are staying with Global Payments, ask them to confirm the records were moved into TransIT and spot check a few customers. If you are leaving, ask for an encrypted, PCI-compliant export sent to your new gateway's vault. Card data is never sent to you as a spreadsheet, and you should not accept it that way.

If stored cards cannot be recovered, the fallback is to email affected customers a secure payment link from the new system so they re-enter their cards once.

### Recurring billing schedules

This is the one that costs money quietly. If a recurring schedule did not make it to TransIT, the charge simply does not run, and nobody gets an error. Before your Transaction Express access ends, write down every active schedule: customer, amount, frequency and next date. After the move, check that each one exists on the new platform and that the next cycle actually charged. Our guide to [how recurring billing works inside a gateway](/gateways/recurring-billing) covers what to look for.

## Your options now

You are changing platforms either way. The question is whether you change only the software or also the provider and the price.

| Path | Effort | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Follow Global Payments to TransIT and WebPASS | Low: new login, staff retraining, check integrations | Your processing rate is fair, or Global Payments re-prices you to keep you, and your software supports TransIT |
| Independent gateway such as [accept.blue](/gateways/accept-blue) or [NMI](/gateways/nmi) on a new interchange-plus merchant account | Medium: new account, stored-card export, recurring rebuilt | Your pricing is old, you want one person to call, or you want dual pricing at the counter on the same account |
| Authorize.net direct | Medium | You want published pricing and the widest plugin catalog |

Both accept.blue and NMI include a [virtual terminal for phone and mail orders](/gateways/virtual-terminal), a tokenized customer vault, recurring billing and ACH. Both can import an encrypted card export from another gateway. For what each costs all-in, see our [payment gateway pricing comparison](/gateways/pricing).

If your website, invoicing software or practice system posted payments to Transaction Express, check it now. Integrations built on TXP were moved to a TransIT proxy in 2023. Ask your software vendor whether they support TransIT directly and plan to keep doing so, or whether they connect to accept.blue or NMI. That one answer often decides the path.

## Why this is the moment to re-price

Many Transaction Express users came over from TransFirst years ago and are still on the pricing they signed then. TransFirst-era accounts commonly show tiered pricing, with qualified, mid-qualified and non-qualified rates, plus monthly, PCI and annual fees added over time. Keyed virtual terminal sales usually land in the higher tiers. Effective rates above 3% are not unusual on these statements.

Global Payments has also been raising prices this year. Per Merchant Cost Consulting's tracker of Global Payments notices (published September 24, 2026), Global Payments raised its Infrastructure Upgrade Fee to $499 in June 2026, raised base discount rates on August 1, 2026, announced another discount rate increase on select accounts as of October 1, 2026, and notified merchants of a new $529 Annual System Enhancement Fee on October 2026 statements. Not every account received every change, which is why your own statement is the only reliable number.

Here is the logic. You are already paying the cost of a platform change: a new login, retraining, and checking your stored cards and recurring schedules. Moving to a new provider adds a merchant application and a stored-card export on top of that. If your rate is stale, the savings usually cover that extra effort within a month or two. If Global Payments offers you a competitive interchange-plus rate to stay, take it and follow the migration. Either way, look at the numbers before you decide. ## How a move works with us

If the numbers say move, this is the sequence. It is the same one we use for merchants [switching from Authorize.net](/gateways/switch-from-authorize-net), with the export request pointed at Global Payments.

1. **Statement review.** We compute your effective rate and show the cost of staying on TransIT next to the cost of moving. If staying is cheaper, we tell you.
2. **Check your software.** We confirm your cart, invoicing tool or practice software connects to the new gateway before anything is signed.
3. **Board the new account.** Merchant account on interchange-plus, gateway set up, virtual terminal users created. Usually two to five business days.
4. **Request the stored-card export.** We help you ask Global Payments, through 800-543-5327, for an encrypted export of your customer and wallet records to the new gateway's vault, and we track it.
5. **Import and rebuild recurring.** Stored cards are imported and mapped. Recurring schedules are recreated to start after the last charge on the old platform, so nobody is billed twice or skipped.
6. **Test live, then cut over.** A few real low-value sales and a refund. When deposits arrive correctly, the new gateway becomes your only one.

Most moves take three to five weeks end to end, with most of that time spent waiting on the card export. You keep processing on your current account the whole time. If you are inside your 30-day TransIT window, we usually suggest activating TransIT anyway so you can keep processing while the move finishes.
