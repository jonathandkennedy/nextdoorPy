Credit card processing fees are the third or fourth biggest expense for most small businesses, after payroll, rent and inventory, and the one owners understand least. The quoted rate is rarely the rate you pay. This guide breaks a card fee into its three layers, shows the 2026 ranges by card type and pricing model, lists what the big providers publish, and explains how to read your own number. The calculator above does the arithmetic once you know which figures to enter.

Every number below is either published by the card networks or the provider, or worked out from those published figures. Where we are estimating, we say so.

## How much are credit card processing fees?

For a typical US small business in 2026, total processing costs land between 2% and 3.5% of card sales. Where you fall depends on four things:

1. **How cards are taken.** In person (tap, chip, swipe) is cheaper than online, keyed or invoiced, because the fraud risk is lower.
2. **Which cards your customers carry.** Debit is cheapest. Premium rewards and business cards are the most expensive.
3. **Your pricing model.** Interchange-plus, flat rate, tiered, membership or dual pricing.
4. **The extra fees.** Monthly, PCI, statement, gateway and annual fees can add 0.2% to 0.5% to a small merchant's effective rate.

For scale: the Nilson Report puts total US merchant processing fees at $198.25 billion in 2025, up from $187.20 billion in 2024.

## The three layers of every card fee

Every card transaction pays three parties. Only one of them sets a price you can negotiate.

| Layer | Who gets it | Who sets it | 2026 range | Negotiable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interchange | The cardholder's bank (issuer) | Visa, Mastercard, Discover publish the tables; Amex sets its own | About 0.05% + 22¢ (big-bank debit) to about 2.8% + 10¢ (keyed business cards) | No |
| Card-brand assessments | Visa, Mastercard, Discover, Amex | The networks | Roughly 0.13% to 0.15% of volume plus small per-transaction network fees | No |
| Processor markup | Your processor and any reseller | Your processor | 0.20% to 1.5% plus 5¢ to 30¢, plus monthly fees | Yes |

On a fair deal, interchange is most of the total, often 70% or more. It is the same whether you process with a bank, Square or us. That is why two providers quoting "lower rates" can only be competing on the third layer.

On interchange-plus pricing you see all three layers on the statement. On flat-rate and tiered pricing the first two are hidden inside one number, and you cannot tell what the processor is keeping. The statement walk-through in [how to read a merchant statement](/guides/how-to-read-a-merchant-statement) shows where each layer sits on the page.

## Interchange fees by card type and channel in 2026

Interchange tables run to hundreds of categories. These are the ranges that matter for a small business, based on Visa's and Mastercard's published US rates as summarized by processors in 2026. Add assessments and markup on top.

| Card and channel | Typical interchange | On a $45 sale |
|---|---|---|
| Debit from a large bank (over $10 billion in assets), in person or online | 0.05% + 22¢ (Durbin-regulated) | About 24¢ (0.5%) |
| Debit from a small bank or credit union, in person | About 0.80% + 15¢ | About 51¢ (1.1%) |
| Standard consumer credit, in person | About 1.5% to 1.65% + 10¢ | 78¢ to 84¢ (about 1.8%) |
| Premium rewards credit (Visa Signature Preferred, Mastercard World Elite), in person | About 2.0% to 2.1% + 10¢ | $1.00 to $1.05 (about 2.3%) |
| Consumer credit, online or keyed | About 1.8% to 2.5% + 10¢ | 91¢ to $1.23 |
| Business, corporate and purchasing cards | About 2.2% to 2.5% + 10¢ in person, 2.7% to 2.8% + 10¢ keyed | $1.09 to $1.36 |
| American Express (OptBlue, through a processor) | Roughly 1.5% to 3% + 10¢ by industry and ticket size, plus about 0.15% in network fees | Varies |

Three things to take from the table:

- **Debit is cheap for the processor.** A regulated debit card costs about 24¢ in interchange on a $45 sale. On a flat 2.6% + 15¢ plan you pay $1.32 for the same transaction. That spread is the provider's margin.
- **Card-not-present costs more.** Online, keyed and invoiced payments run about 0.4% to 0.6% higher than the same card tapped in person.
- **Business cards are the most expensive.** B2B sellers can lower the cost on qualifying corporate and purchasing cards by sending Level 2 and Level 3 data. See [Level 3 processing](/gateways/level-3-processing).

Two things could move interchange in the next year. A revised Visa and Mastercard settlement with merchants received preliminary court approval on June 9, 2026. If it gets final approval, it would cut average credit interchange by 0.10 percentage points for five years and cap standard consumer card rates at 1.25%. Separately, the Durbin debit cap of 21¢ + 0.05% plus a 1¢ fraud adjustment is being challenged in federal court, and a 2023 Federal Reserve proposal to lower it has not been finalized. Neither changes your bill today.

## Pricing models, and what each costs on the same volume

Processors package the three layers in five ways. Here is one business priced each way: $25,000 a month in card sales, in person, $45 average sale, about 556 transactions. For the interchange-based models I assume blended interchange and assessments of 1.65% of volume, which is typical for an in-person card mix with about 40% debit. Your mix will differ.

| Pricing model | How it works | Example rate | Monthly cost | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interchange-plus | Actual interchange and assessments passed through, plus a fixed disclosed markup | Interchange + 0.25% + 8¢, $15 monthly | About $534 | 2.14% |
| Membership (subscription) | Interchange passed through at cost, plus a flat monthly fee and a small per-item fee | Interchange + 7¢, $79 monthly | About $530 | 2.12% |
| Flat rate | One rate for every card | 2.3% + 10¢ (Clover direct) | About $631 plus software | 2.52% |
| Flat rate | One rate for every card | 2.6% + 15¢ (Square) | About $733 | 2.93% |
| Tiered | Transactions sorted into qualified, mid-qualified and non-qualified buckets at rates the processor sets | 1.69% / 2.49% / 3.39%, 12¢, $80 in monthly fees | About $777 | 3.11% |
| Dual pricing | Cash price and card price both posted; the card price covers acceptance | Card price 3.5% to 3.99% above cash | $0 processing | 0% |

**Interchange-plus** is the transparent model. You see the real cost and the markup separately, and the markup does not move when your card mix changes. A good small-business markup in 2026 is 0.20% to 0.40% plus 5¢ to 10¢.

**Membership pricing** passes interchange at cost and charges a monthly subscription. It beats interchange-plus at higher volume and loses at low volume, because the monthly fee is fixed.

**Flat rate** is simple and predictable, and it is how Square, Stripe, Clover direct, PayPal and QuickBooks price. You pay the same on a debit card as on a corporate card, so the more debit your customers use, the more you overpay.

**Tiered** is the model to leave. The low "qualified" rate that sold the account usually applies to a third or less of volume, and most rewards cards land in the expensive buckets.

**Dual pricing** moves the cost of acceptance to the card price, so card payers fund it and the business nets the cash price on every sale. How it differs from surcharging and from a plain [cash discount program](/dual-pricing/cash-discount-program) matters for compliance, especially in California.

## Monthly and hidden fees

The rate is not the bill. These are the lines that push a 2.5% quote to a 3.3% effective rate:

| Fee | Typical amount | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| PCI non-compliance | $20 to $99 a month | Complete the annual self-assessment questionnaire (about 20 minutes) and it stops |
| PCI compliance or "security" | $5 to $30 a month | Charged even when compliant. Margin. Ask for it removed. |
| Statement or online access | $5 to $15 a month | Margin |
| Batch or settlement | 10¢ to 35¢ per day | Small, but $3 to $10 a month |
| Gateway | $10 to $30 a month | Fair if you use one. Make sure you are not paying for two. |
| Annual or membership | $99 to $199 a year | Often billed in a slow month |
| Monthly minimum | Around $25 | Hits seasonal businesses |
| Chargeback | $15 to $35 each | Stripe charges $15, PayPal $20. Avoidable only by preventing disputes. |
| Equipment lease | $30 to $90 a month for 48 months | Non-cancellable. The most expensive line on most statements. |
| Early termination | $295 to $595 | Ask before you sign, not when you leave |

Also watch the statement footer. Rate increases are usually announced there with a date, and silence counts as consent.

## Effective rate: the only number that compares offers

**Effective rate = total fees for the month ÷ total card volume for the month.**

Include every fee: percentage, per-item, monthly, PCI, gateway, lease. Example from our statement guide: a restaurant with $61,000 in monthly card volume paid $1,888 in total fees on tiered pricing. $1,888 ÷ $61,000 = 3.09%. The "1.69%" that sold the account applied to less than a third of its volume.

### Using your statement in the calculator above

The calculator above takes five numbers. Here is where each one is on a typical statement:

1. **Monthly card sales.** Total card volume or "gross sales" for the month, usually on page one.
2. **Average sale.** Total volume divided by the number of transactions. If the statement shows 1,600 transactions on $61,000, enter $38.
3. **Your percentage rate.** On flat-rate pricing, the rate you were quoted (2.6, 2.9). On interchange-plus, add your markup to about 1.65 for an in-person mix or 2.1 for an online mix as a rough interchange estimate. On tiered pricing, the honest shortcut is to enter your effective rate here and put zero in the next two fields.
4. **Per-transaction fee.** The cents charged on each sale, such as 10, 15 or 30. Include any separate authorization fee.
5. **Monthly account fees.** Add statement, PCI, gateway, annual (divided by 12) and lease fees.

The calculator returns your monthly cost, your effective rate and your annual cost, then shows the same volume at Clover direct, Square in person and Stripe online, and at dual pricing. If your effective rate from the calculator is lower than the one on your statement, there are fees you have not found yet.

## How to lower your processing fees, in order of impact

1. **Switch to dual pricing.** Post a cash price and a card price, and the card price covers the cost of acceptance. Your processing cost goes to zero on every card, credit and debit alike. It is the only option on this list that removes the cost rather than shrinking it. See [how dual pricing works](/dual-pricing).
2. **Leave tiered pricing for interchange-plus.** Typical savings are 0.5% to 1.2% of volume. On $50,000 a month that is $250 to $600 a month.
3. **Leave flat rate once you pass about $10,000 to $15,000 a month.** A flat 2.6% to 2.9% overcharges you on every debit card. Interchange-plus is usually cheaper above this volume, more so the more debit you take.
4. **Get out of the equipment lease.** Ask for the payoff and end date. Buying out a lease and taking free placement is often cheaper than finishing it.
5. **Strip the junk fees.** Complete the PCI questionnaire. Ask in writing for statement, annual, regulatory and "security" fees to be removed. Most processors will remove some to keep the account.
6. **Take cards in person when you can.** Tap or dip instead of keying. Card-present interchange is about 0.4% to 0.6% lower, and fraud is lower.
7. **Send Level 2 and Level 3 data on B2B sales.** On qualifying corporate and purchasing cards this cuts interchange meaningfully.
8. **Steer large invoices to ACH.** A flat or capped ACH fee is far cheaper than 2.99% on a $10,000 invoice.

Surcharging is not on this list for California businesses. It is capped at 3% by Visa, cannot be applied to debit or prepaid cards, needs 30 days' notice to your acquirer, and is restricted under California law. The differences are laid out in [dual pricing vs surcharging vs cash discount](/dual-pricing/dual-pricing-vs-surcharging-vs-cash-discount).

## What is a good credit card processing rate?

Honest ranges, all-in effective rate including every fee, for a small business on traditional pricing in 2026:

| Business type | Good | Fair | Overpaying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant or retail, mostly in person, average sale over $20 | Under 2.2% | 2.2% to 2.7% | Over 3% |
| Coffee shop or quick service, small tickets under $15 | Under 2.6% | 2.6% to 3.2% | Over 3.5% |
| Salon, auto repair or medical, in person, higher tickets | Under 2.3% | 2.3% to 2.8% | Over 3% |
| Ecommerce, online only | Under 2.5% | 2.5% to 3.0% | Over 3.4% |
| Contractor or B2B, invoiced, many business cards | Under 2.6% without Level 3 | 2.6% to 3.1% | Over 3.4% |
| Any business on dual pricing | 0% | n/a | n/a |

Small tickets run higher because the per-transaction fee is a bigger share of each sale: 15¢ is 0.3% of a $50 sale and 3% of a $5 coffee. Card-not-present runs higher because interchange is higher. Business-card-heavy merchants run higher because commercial interchange is the most expensive.

These are our working benchmarks from reviewing merchant statements, not a published industry standard. If you land in the right-hand column, the fix is usually one of the first three steps above.

## The bottom line

Card fees are not a mystery once you split them into three layers. The networks set interchange and assessments, and nobody can lower those for you. Everything else is your processor's price, and it is negotiable. Put your statement numbers into the calculator above, compare your effective rate with the table, and decide whether to renegotiate, switch to interchange-plus, or stop paying the cost at all with dual pricing.
