QuickBooks Payments is convenient. The payment lands on the right invoice, the deposit reconciles itself, and nobody has to think about it. The cost of that convenience is a flat percentage that does not go down as you grow, and it is highest on exactly the transactions service businesses do most: emailed invoices paid by card. This page lists the current rates, works through what they cost, and shows the options that keep QuickBooks and change the processing.

## QuickBooks Payments rates, 2026

From Intuit's payment rates page, stated as accurate as of April 30, 2026:

| Payment method | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cards and digital wallets, online or invoiced | 2.99% | Most invoice payments land here |
| Cards in person, swiped or tapped | 2.5% | Requires a QuickBooks card reader |
| Cards keyed in | 3.5% | Phone orders, manual entry |
| ACH bank transfer | 1% | $1 minimum, no cap |
| Buy now, pay later | 2.99% | |
| Instant deposit | 1.75% | On top of the transaction rate |
| Payment dispute protection | From 0.99% | Optional |

There is no monthly fee on pay-as-you-go. Intuit invites businesses over $2,500 a month to call for up to 25% off, which tells you the list rates have room in them. These rates apply to QuickBooks Online; QuickBooks Desktop merchant accounts have historically had their own schedule, and Intuit has been moving Desktop users toward Online.

## What it costs on real volume

Three service businesses, all invoicing through QuickBooks, paid by emailed invoice.

| | Small: $12,000 a month, 80% card | Mid: $40,000 a month, 70% card | Large: $120,000 a month, 50% card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card fees at 2.99% | $287 | $837 | $1,794 |
| ACH fees at 1% | $24 | $120 | $600 |
| Monthly total | $311 | $957 | $2,394 |
| Annual | $3,700 | $11,500 | $28,700 |
| Effective rate | 2.59% | 2.39% | 2.00% |

Instant deposit at 1.75% on top of that turns the mid-size example into about $1,650 a month if every payment is rushed.

The ACH line is worth a second look. QuickBooks charges 1% with no cap. A contractor collecting a $25,000 progress payment by ACH pays $250 for a bank transfer that costs the processor a fraction of a dollar.

## The three ways to lower it

### 1. Ask Intuit for the discount

If you process over $2,500 a month, call and ask for the reduced rates. Twenty-five percent off 2.99% is about 2.24%. It is the easiest first step and it costs nothing. It does not fix the uncapped ACH or the 3.5% keyed rate.

### 2. Keep QuickBooks, replace the processing with interchange-plus

Third-party invoicing and payment platforms connect to QuickBooks Online and Desktop, present a pay link on the invoice, and record the payment against the invoice when it arrives. The processing behind them is priced on interchange plus a fixed markup rather than a flat 2.99%.

For an invoiced card mix, interchange-plus typically lands between 2.1% and 2.5% all-in, with ACH at a flat $1 to $3 per item or a low percentage with a cap. On the mid example above that is roughly $600 to $700 a month instead of $957, and the ACH savings grow with ticket size.

We set this up with Hyfin: text-to-pay and email invoicing, recurring billing, ACH and card, two-way sync with QuickBooks. The invoice still comes from your QuickBooks data. The customer still clicks a link. The difference is on your statement.

### 3. Put the card cost on the payer with a dual-priced invoice

For B2B and trade businesses, the invoice can show two amounts: a bank-transfer price and a card price that includes the cost of acceptance. The customer picks. This is dual pricing applied to invoicing, and it is the same model as two prices at a register. The business nets the bank-transfer price either way and its card processing cost goes to zero.

Two rules matter. Both prices must be visible on the invoice before the payer chooses, and the card amount is the price charged, not a base price plus a "fee" line. Adding a manual "3% processing fee" line in QuickBooks is a surcharge, with the surcharge cap, the debit prohibition, and state restrictions that come with it. The differences are laid out in [dual pricing vs surcharging vs cash discount](/dual-pricing/dual-pricing-vs-surcharging-vs-cash-discount).

## What stays the same when you switch the processing

Your QuickBooks file, chart of accounts, customers and invoices. Your invoice template and numbering. Bank reconciliation, because payments still post to the invoice and deposits still match. What changes is the pay link on the invoice, the processor depositing the money, and the line on your P&L for merchant fees.

## When QuickBooks Payments is the right choice

Under about $5,000 a month, paid mostly by ACH on small invoices, with no appetite for a second login. The convenience is worth more than the 1%.
