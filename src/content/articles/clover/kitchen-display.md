A kitchen printer is a rail of paper that gets greasy, torn and reordered by hand. A kitchen display shows every ticket in order, times it, and lets the line bump it when it is done. Clover's KDS is built for the heat and connects to everything Clover sends to the kitchen. This page covers the hardware, the cost, and whether your kitchen needs it.

## What it does

- **Ticket organization** by time, status and priority, with elapsed timers and color changes as tickets age.
- **Routing by station.** Grill, fry, salad, bar each see their items; the whole ticket can show on expo.
- **Bump and recall.** Done items bumped off the screen, recalled if needed.
- **Expo mode.** One view for cooking, another for assembly, bagging and pickup verification.
- **Runner tickets.** Printed from expo for delivery to tables when you run food.
- **Coursing.** Fired courses from Clover Dining appear when fired, not before.
- **Online and delivery orders** with pickup or delivery times, so the line sequences them against walk-ups.

A taco counter with one cook is fine on a printer. A fast-casual line with grill, assembly and expo doing 300 orders a day, plus delivery, wants KDS.

## Setup

Screens mounted where each station looks, routing by category to the right screen, expo screen for the pass if you run one, bump bar on the 24-inch if hands are dirty, timers and color thresholds set to your ticket-time targets, runner ticket printer at expo if you run food. Test tickets from the register, the kiosk and an online order before service. The quick-service layout with KDS is on [Clover for quick service](/clover/for-quick-service).
