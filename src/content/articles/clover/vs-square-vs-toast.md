## Where each one wins

**Square.** Under about $5,000 a month. New businesses that do not know their volume. Anyone who wants an account in ten minutes and one login for POS, appointments, invoices and payroll. Very small retail and services.

**Toast.** Multi-location restaurant groups. Restaurants that want payroll, scheduling, tips management and inventory in one vendor and will pay for it in processing. Complex kitchens with Toast's KDS and routing.

**Clover.** Single-location restaurants, bars and cafes above $5,000 a month that care about the processing line. Retail with barcodes. Service businesses that want a real POS. Any business that wants to choose its processor, run dual pricing, and keep month-to-month terms. Read the dealer contract; the risk on the Clover side is the seller, not the product.

## The risks

Square: aggregator holds and closures after the fact. Toast: processing lock-in and multi-year terms; nothing is free, the $0 hardware is in the rate. Clover: a bad dealer with leases, ETFs and teaser rates. The eight questions that protect you from the last one are on [Clover reseller vs direct](/clover/reseller-vs-direct).

## The decision

Brand new or tiny: Square. Multi-location or one-vendor: Toast. Everyone else with a counter or a dining room: Clover through a dealer who puts terms on one page. The two-way comparisons with more detail are [Clover vs Square](/clover/vs-square) and [Clover vs Toast](/clover/vs-toast).
