Auto repair is a high-ticket business paying retail-style card fees. A shop doing $80,000 a month in cards at 2.8% pays about $2,200 a month for the privilege of getting paid. The fix is partly hardware, partly workflow (get the customer to pay before pickup), and mostly pricing. Here is the Clover setup for a service counter and how it fits with shop software.

## The setup

| Position | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Service counter | Clover Mini (screen toward the customer), or Compact if invoicing lives in shop software | Tap, tip if you take them, dual pricing display, receipts |
| Lot, pickups, mobile | Flex on LTE | Payment at the car or at a customer's location |
| Estimates and invoices | Shop management software | Estimates, parts, labor, invoice |
| Pay before pickup | Text-to-pay link (invoicing tool with QuickBooks sync, or Clover's virtual terminal invoicing) | Customer pays from their phone |
| Deposits on parts | Virtual terminal or Flex; stored card with consent | Charge the deposit, balance at pickup |

Hardware placed at no charge for qualifying shops. Plan: Essentials at $14.95 for the virtual terminal and invoicing, or Payments at $0 with a Compact.

## Workflow that cuts the fees and the line

1. Estimate approved by text.
2. Text-to-pay link sent when the car is ready; most customers pay before they arrive.
3. Pickup is keys and a printed or emailed receipt.
4. Walk-ups pay at the Mini; customers who cannot come in pay on the Flex at delivery.

Text-to-pay is card-not-present. On interchange-plus that is about 2.2% to 2.5%; on a dual-priced invoice the customer picks bank transfer or card price and the shop's cost is zero. The contractor version of this setup, with QuickBooks sync, is on [contractors and service businesses](/industries/contractors-and-service).

## Fleet and commercial cards

Fleet, corporate and purchasing cards carry the highest interchange. Entering the tax amount and a customer or PO number in the virtual terminal (Level 2) qualifies them for lower rates on interchange-plus pricing. Under dual pricing the point is moot for the shop; the card price covers it. For a shop with heavy commercial accounts, Level 3 through a gateway is worth a look; see [Level 3 processing](/gateways/level-3-processing).

## Dual pricing on big tickets

The estimate and the counter sign show the cash price and the card price; the Mini shows both totals before the tap. A $900 repair is $900 cash or debit-as-cash where configured, $935.91 by card at 3.99%. Customers choose. Shops running it report that a meaningful share switch to debit or check, and the shop's processing bill goes to zero. The rules and the difference from a surcharge (which cannot apply to debit and is restricted in California) are on [dual pricing vs surcharging vs cash discount](/dual-pricing/dual-pricing-vs-surcharging-vs-cash-discount).

Shops that want one price on the wall get interchange-plus instead, around 2.0% to 2.3% all-in for a typical repair-shop mix.

## Cost

Mini or Compact and a Flex placed. Essentials $14.95 a month or Payments $0. Processing $0 under dual pricing or interchange-plus. Month to month, fee schedule on one page. Compare it to a leased terminal at 2.8% plus $45 a month, which is what we see on most shop statements; the method is on the [statement guide](/guides/how-to-read-a-merchant-statement).
